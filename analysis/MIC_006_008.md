# High-Risk Verse Analysis: Micah 6:8 (HR016)

- **Reference**: Micah 6:8 (`HR016`, Category: 암송 / Memory Verse)
- **BSB Source Text**:
  > He has shown you, O man, what is good.
  > And what does the LORD require of you
  > but to act justly, to love mercy,
  > and to walk humbly with your God?

## 1. Structural & Rhetorical Analysis
- **Context**: Set within God's covenant lawsuit (*rib*) against Israel (Micah 6:1–8). After Israel asks in escalating hyperbole what sacrifices might appease the LORD—even thousands of rams, ten thousand rivers of oil, or a firstborn child (vv. 6–7)—the prophet answers with a sharp, three-part ethical summary of covenant faithfulness.
- **Line 1**: "He has shown you, O man, what is good." — Appeals to shared, already-revealed covenant instruction rather than secret ritual formulas.
- **Line 2 (Rhetorical Question)**: "And what does the LORD require of you..." — Introduces the three infinitive clauses that define true devotion.
- **Triad of Requirements**:
  1. **to act justly** (*asot mishpat*): Practicing fairness, social justice, and right dealing in community life.
  2. **to love mercy** (*ahavat chesed*): Cherishing steadfast, loyal love and active compassion toward others.
  3. **to walk humbly with your God** (*hatzne'a lechet im Eloheicha*): Living in daily, unpretentious, obedient fellowship with God.

## 2. Key-Term & Readability Decisions (SKV)
- **LORD** → **주** (per glossary).
- **to act justly** → **공의를 행하고** (clear and faithful to *mishpat*).
- **to love mercy** → **자비를 사랑하며** (clearer for today's middle-school readers than archaic "인자를 사랑하며").
- **to walk humbly with your God** → **겸손하게 네 하나님과 함께 걷는 것** (preserves the relational metaphor of walking with God in natural modern Korean).

## 3. Target Korean Translation (SKV)
> 사람아, 무엇이 선한 것인지 주께서 이미 네게 보여 주셨다.
> 주께서 네게 요구하시는 것이 무엇이냐?
> 오직 공의를 행하고, 자비를 사랑하며,
> 겸손하게 네 하나님과 함께 걷는 것이 아니냐?
