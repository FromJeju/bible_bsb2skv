# Numbers 6:24–26 (HR003) — Aaron's Blessing (Priestly Benediction)

## Source Text (BSB)
- **v. 24**: "May the LORD bless you / and keep you;"
- **v. 25**: "may the LORD cause His face to shine upon you / and be gracious to you;"
- **v. 26**: "may the LORD lift up His countenance toward you / and give you peace."

## Structural & Poetic Analysis
- Three progressive poetic couplets (3-beat / 5-beat / 7-beat progression in Hebrew), each marked `is_poetry: true` with `\n` line breaks preserved.
- Each couplet begins with the covenant name **LORD** (`주`, glossary-enforced) in jussive/optative mood ("May the LORD...").
- Parallelism moves from general protection (**bless** / **keep**), to relational favor (**cause His face to shine** / **be gracious**), to full presence and wholeness (**lift up His countenance** / **give you peace**).

## Key Terms & Middle-Schooler Korean Rendering
- **LORD**: `주` (glossary mandatory).
- **bless you / keep you**: `복을 주시고 / 지켜 주시기를` — clear, natural, avoiding archaic `~복을 주시며 지키시기를 원하노라`.
- **cause His face to shine upon you / be gracious to you**: `얼굴을 비추어 주시고 / 은혜를 베푸시기를` — preserves the vivid metaphor of light/favor while staying immediately clear.
- **lift up His countenance toward you / give you peace**: `얼굴을 들어 너희를 바라보시고 / 평화를 주시기를` — "countenance" rendered naturally as `얼굴` (facing someone in favor/attention) rather than archaic `안색` or `얼굴빛`, paired with `평화` for `peace`.
- **Poetic cadence**: Ending each couplet with `~주시기를` (following v. 23's framing `이렇게 복을 빌어 주어라: "..."`) preserves the rhythmic, liturgical blessing structure in modern Korean without stiff endings (`~원하노라`).
